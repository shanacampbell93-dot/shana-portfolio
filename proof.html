How it works

What's a fingerprint?

Your device runs your file through a maths function called SHA-256, which turns it into a unique 64-character code. Change even one pixel or letter and the code changes completely. The code can't be turned back into your file, so nobody can see your work from it.

What goes on the blockchain?

Only the fingerprint, mixed with a random value first so nobody can tell which one is yours. It's sent to the free, open-source OpenTimestamps servers, which bundle it with thousands of others into a single Bitcoin transaction. That's why it costs nothing. Your file, title and name are never sent.

How do I prove it later?

Keep your original file safe and unchanged, with its .ots proof file. Anyone can drop both into opentimestamps.org and see the date Bitcoin recorded it, without needing to trust me or this site.

Is this legal proof of copyright?

In the UK, copyright belongs to you automatically when you create something. This doesn't replace that, and it isn't legal advice. It's a dated, tamper-proof record that your exact file existed at that time. It doesn't prove who made it on its own, so keep your sketches and drafts too. In 2025 a French court accepted blockchain timestamps as part of the evidence in a design-copying case. For disputes, speak to a solicitor.

What happened to the Sepolia version?

The first version ran on my own Solidity smart contract on Ethereum's Sepolia test network. Test networks get shut down eventually, so proofs there wouldn't last. This version anchors to Bitcoin instead, which is permanent and free to use.
